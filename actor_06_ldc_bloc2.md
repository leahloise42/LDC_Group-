# Actor 6 — LDC Group (Least Developed Countries)

## Bloc 2 — African & Least Developed Coalition

> **Keep this document private.** Your mandate describes who you are and what you want from the agreement. You may tell other groups what you want — you may not show them this document.

**Who you are:** You represent the 44 poorest countries in the world — Bangladesh, Nepal, Mali, Mozambique, and others. Your countries contribute almost nothing to the climate problem yet are on the frontline of its consequences.

**What you want:**

- 1.5°C must remain the operative target
- Climate finance must be grants, not loans — your countries cannot take on more debt
- Loss and Damage (compensation for irreversible climate harms) must be properly funded as its own standalone mechanism, not absorbed into existing aid
- Any global welfare measure must first ask whether the poorest regions are above a basic living standard — not just whether the global total is rising
- **Red line:** A system where climate finance comes primarily as loans
- **Minimum condition:** Grant-based adaptation and Loss and Damage funding with a defined minimum level

**Your welfare lens:** Your mandate calls for a welfare framework that **treats falling below a basic minimum as the first priority**. The sufficiency floor is approximately $0.456k/capita/yr. Show whether `rsas` and `rsaf` are at risk of falling below it — a framework that maximises the global total has no concept of a floor and can declare success while your regions remain in deprivation.

*(Regions likely relevant to your case: `rsas`, `rsaf`.)*

**Some analytical directions to consider:**

1. Does `consumption_per_capita` for both `rsas` and `rsaf` stay above the sufficiency floor under your preferred policy and across SSP scenarios?
2. At what point in the simulation, and under which scenarios, does the floor constraint bind?
3. Under a framework that maximises the global total, can aggregate welfare increase while `rsas` or `rsaf` remain below the floor?
